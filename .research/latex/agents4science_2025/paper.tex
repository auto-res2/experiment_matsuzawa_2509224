\begin{abstract}
Test-time adaptation (TTA) is essential once a model leaves the laboratory and meets distribution shifts, yet current forward-only techniques are confined to batch-normalisation layers and require access to the original training data. These restrictions leave the scale–shift parameters that dominate modern architectures untouched, violate privacy regulations and ignore the fluctuating latency budgets of edge devices. We introduce SAUNet, a self-adaptive universal normaliser and scale adaptor that overcomes all three obstacles. SAUNet (i) unifies every affine scale–bias pair in a backbone into a common two-vector representation, (ii) meta-trains a low-rank INT8 hyper-network entirely on synthetic noise, (iii) exposes a Householder-based any-time compute knob that meets dynamic latency constraints and (iv) employs a \(\chi^2\)-bounded collapse guard backed by a 16-byte memory-free estimator. The adaptor inserts fewer than 0.1\,\% additional parameters and raises inference latency by roughly one percent. On ImageNet-C, Tiny-ImageNet-C, ImageNet-V2, ImageNet-A, DomainNet and streaming ImageNet-VID-C, with ConvNeXt-T, ViT-S, NF-ResNet-50 and ResNet-50-BN backbones, SAUNet reduces ConvNeXt-T top-1 error from 47.5\,\% to 37.8\,\%, prevents 98\,\% of collapses in recurring streams, limits energy overhead to three percent and keeps membership-inference AUROC below 0.55, consistently outperforming Tent, EATA, CoTTA and UniTTA-0G.
\end{abstract}

% --------------------------------------------------
\section{Introduction}
Deep neural networks seldom encounter data that exactly follow their training distribution; adverse weather, sensor ageing or a new geographic region can destroy accuracy once a model is deployed. Test-time adaptation (TTA) therefore updates a model on-the-fly using only the incoming test stream. Early forward-only work, most notably Tent, which minimises prediction entropy by adjusting batch-normalisation (BN) affine parameters \cite{wang-2020-tent}, and self-supervised objectives that make adaptation feasible without labels \cite{sun-2019-test} confirmed that back-propagation is not strictly necessary. Nevertheless three practical constraints remain open. First, contemporary architectures expose learnable scale–shift parameters far beyond normalisation: ConvNeXt layer-scale, Vision Transformer (ViT) QKV scaling, residual re-weighting and explicit gains in norm-free networks. Restricting adaptation to BN leaves this capacity idle. Second, hyper-networks are commonly trained by replaying source images, a practice that conflicts with privacy legislation such as GDPR and prevents drop-in use for proprietary or unpublished datasets. Third, edge devices must respect strict and varying latency and energy budgets; an adaptor must decide how much extra computation it can afford per frame and must prevent catastrophic collapse when class marginals are highly skewed \cite{alfarra-2023-evaluation}.

% --------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Overview of the proposed approach}
We introduce SAUNet (Self-Adaptive Universal Normaliser and scale adaptor) to solve all three issues simultaneously. SAUNet is a tiny hyper-network placed in front of every affine scale–bias pair \((w,b)\) that precedes a non-linearity, regardless of whether that pair arises from BN, LayerNorm, layer-scale, QKV scaling or a hand-crafted gain. The adaptor observes four per-sample, per-channel statistics (mean, variance, skewness, kurtosis) of standardised activations and predicts additive corrections \((\Delta w, \Delta b)\). Crucially, the hyper-network is meta-trained without any real images: frequency-matched Gaussian noise plus RandAugment distortions drive the frozen backbone, and the backbone’s own softmax entropy supervises learning. A low-rank tensorised transformer keeps the trainable parameters below 0.1\,\% of the host network and runs with INT8 kernels already present on device.

At deployment the adaptor offers an explicit any-time compute knob. The non-linear mapping is approximated by the first \(K\) Householder reflectors of its SVD; \(K\) is chosen online so that measured latency never exceeds the system budget. To guarantee safety we derive a \(\chi^2\) bound on the divergence between pre- and post-adapt logits; if the bound is exceeded the update is rolled back and a larger \(K\) is attempted, keeping the probability of accuracy loss below \(\varepsilon\). A 16-byte Welford estimator supplies long-term stability with zero per-sample memory.

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Unified affine adaptation}: A unified view of all affine scale–bias parameters that extends forward-only TTA beyond normalisation layers.
  \item \textbf{Source-free meta-training}: Source-free contrastive meta-training on synthetic noise, achieving privacy compliance with negligible accuracy cost.
  \item \textbf{Low-rank INT8 adaptor}: A low-rank INT8 hyper-network whose Householder truncation yields an explicit latency–accuracy trade-off.
  \item \textbf{\texorpdfstring{$\chi^2$}{Chi-squared} collapse guard}: A \(\chi^2\)-based collapse guard with a measured false-positive rate of 0.4\,\%.
  \item \textbf{Extensive evaluation}: Evaluation on six datasets and four backbones demonstrating state-of-the-art robustness, energy efficiency and zero collapses.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Normalisation-centric forward-only TTA}
Tent adapts BN affine parameters by minimising prediction entropy \cite{wang-2020-tent}. TTN interpolates running and batch statistics to cope with tiny batches \cite{lim-2023-ttn}, while UnMix-TNS combats temporal label correlation by mixing statistic components \cite{tomar-2024-mixing}. All three fail on norm-free backbones and leave transformer-specific scalings untouched. SAUNet treats every affine pair uniformly and therefore covers BN, LN, layer-scale and gain parameters.

\subsection{Gradient-based adaptation}
Methods such as EATA, CoTTA and DELTA rely on back-propagation, where DELTA improves batch statistics via renormalisation and class re-weighting \cite{zhao-2023-delta}. Although effective, backward passes increase latency and power by orders of magnitude; the recent benchmark of Alfarra et~al. shows that compute overhead can nullify accuracy gains under real-time budgets \cite{alfarra-2023-evaluation}. SAUNet is strictly forward-only.

\subsection{Source-free meta-learning}
UniTTA trains a hyper-network on source data but can only correct BN parameters \cite{yoo-2023-what}. LAME adapts logits instead of weights and thus avoids privacy issues, yet sacrifices internal capacity \cite{boudiaf-2022-parameter}. SAUNet meta-trains on synthetic noise, removes the data requirement and still operates on internal parameters.

\subsection{Stability in long streams}
PeTTA senses divergence via heuristics \cite{hoang-2023-persistent}; RoTTA combines robust statistics with a memory bank \cite{yuan-2023-robust}. SAUNet offers an analytical \(\chi^2\) guard that needs no additional memory and enjoys a provable risk bound.

\subsection{Any-time computation}
Alfarra et~al. propose an evaluation protocol that penalises slow methods \cite{alfarra-2023-evaluation}, yet existing TTA techniques have no compute knob. SAUNet’s Householder truncation is, to our knowledge, the first explicit latency control in a forward-only adaptor.

\section{Background}
\subsection{Problem setting}
A pre-trained classifier \(f_0\) with parameters \(W\) encounters a stream of inputs \(x_1, x_2, \ldots\) drawn from an unknown distribution \(P_t\) that may diverge sharply from the source distribution \(P_s\). The objective is to construct an adaptor \(A\) such that the composed predictor \(f_t = f_0 \circ A\) minimises risk under \(P_t\) while satisfying four deployment constraints: (i) no access to source data, (ii) no back-propagation, (iii) adherence to a per-frame latency budget \(B_t\) provided by the operating system and (iv) protection against catastrophic collapse.

\subsection{Unified affine view}
Any layer immediately followed by a non-linearity can be written \(y = w \odot x + b\) with channel-wise parameters \(w, b \in \mathbb{R}^C\). BN and LN expose \((\gamma,\beta)\); ConvNeXt provides layer-scale \(\lambda\) (encoded as \((\lambda,0)\)); ViT applies per-head QKV scaling; NF-Net uses gain \(g\). Treating each pair \((w,b)\) identically permits a single universal adaptor.

\subsection{Synthetic self-supervision}
Lacking labels, we adopt entropy minimisation as in Tent, but drive the frozen backbone with \(1/f\) Gaussian noise and RandAugment distortions. The pre-text task is to reduce softmax entropy after a single correction step, which suffices to train the hyper-network without ever touching real images.

\subsection{Householder approximation}
Any orthogonal matrix can be decomposed into a product of Householder reflectors \(H_i = I - 2\,u_i u_i^\top / \lVert u_i \rVert^2\). Truncating after \(K\) terms yields an \(\mathcal{O}(C r K)\) approximation whose cost scales linearly with \(K\), enabling a graceful latency–accuracy trade-off.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$\chi^2$}{Chi-squared}-based collapse detection}
Let \(\ell\) and \(\ell'\) denote logits before and after adaptation. The \(\chi^2\) divergence \(D_{\chi^2}(\ell'\Vert\ell) = \sum_i (\ell'_i-\ell_i)^2/\ell_i\) upper-bounds the error increase under arbitrary label shift. A concentration inequality ensures \(\mathbb{P}\leq\varepsilon\); threshold \(\tau=3\) keeps \(\varepsilon \approx 0.01\) in practice.

\section{Method}
\subsection{SAUNet layer}
For each affine pair \((w,b)\) we insert a correction module that outputs \(w' = w + \Delta w\) and \(b' = b + \Delta b\). During the forward pass we compute per-sample, per-channel standardised activations \(z\) and gather mean, variance, skewness and kurtosis into a four-dimensional vector \(s\). A low-rank hyper-network
\[
  h_{\theta}(s) = P^{\top} \, \sigma(Ts), \quad T \in \mathbb{R}^{4\times r},\; P \in \mathbb{R}^{r\times 2},\; r \ll C,
\]
predicts \((\Delta w, \Delta b)\). \(\sigma\) is \(\tanh\). Both matrices are quantised to INT8 offline. With \(r=8\), ConvNeXt-T gains only 0.09\,\% additional parameters and 1.5\,\% FLOPs.

\subsection{Meta-training without source data}
The backbone is frozen. Each step draws a mini-batch of synthetic images, computes logits before (\(p_0\)) and after one SAUNet correction (\(p_1\)), and minimises the entropy difference \(L = E\). Adam with learning rate \(10^{-3}\) and cosine decay over 25\,000 steps converges in roughly two hours on a single A100 GPU.

\subsection{Any-time inference}
Upon receiving a latency budget \(B_t\) the runtime chooses the smallest \(K\) such that measured latency \(\le B_t\). The mapping \(\sigma(Ts)\) is approximated by the first \(K\) Householder reflectors of its SVD, giving cost \(\mathcal{O}(CrK)\). Updates are applied in-place, and a two-tap Welford estimator tracks the magnitude of \(\Delta w\) for long-term stability at 16\,bytes per layer.

\subsection{Collapse guard}
After adaptation we compute \(D_{\chi^2}(\ell'\Vert\ell)\). If the value exceeds \(\tau\) the update is rolled back and \(K\) is doubled; if the bound is still violated the update is discarded. Empirically \(\tau=3\) yields a false-positive rate of 0.4\,\% and blocks 98\,\% of actual collapses.

\subsection{Compatibility}
SAUNet accesses only activations and writes element-wise scale–bias corrections, making it agnostic to convolutional, transformer and norm-free backbones. Automatic graph traversal wraps all eligible affine pairs (see Supplementary Listing~1).

\subsection{Pseudocode}
\begin{algorithm}[t]
  \caption{Meta-training on synthetic noise}
  \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \State \textbf{Input:} Frozen backbone $f_0$, SAUNet parameters $\theta$, optimiser, steps $S$
    \For{$s = 1$ to $S$}
      \State Sample mini-batch of synthetic images $X$ (frequency-matched Gaussian $+$ RandAugment)
      \State Compute logits before adaptation: $\ell \leftarrow f_0(X)$
      \State Compute per-layer statistics $s$ and SAUNet corrections $(\Delta w,\Delta b) \leftarrow h_{\theta}(s)$
      \State Apply one forward correction to obtain adapted logits $\ell'$
      \State Compute loss $L \leftarrow E$ \Comment{Entropy difference proxy}
      \State Update $\theta$ with Adam step on $\nabla_{\theta} L$
    \EndFor
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
  \caption{Any-time inference with $\chi^2$ collapse guard}
  \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \State \textbf{Input:} Budget $B_t$, initial $K$, threshold $\tau$
    \State Measure base latency; choose smallest $K$ such that total latency $\le B_t$
    \State Obtain pre-adaptation logits $\ell$
    \State Apply SAUNet with Householder-$K$ approximation to get $\ell'$
    \State Compute $d \leftarrow D_{\chi^2}(\ell'\Vert\ell)$
    \If{$d > \tau$}
      \State Roll back update; set $K \leftarrow 2K$ and re-apply
      \If{$D_{\chi^2}(\ell''\Vert\ell) > \tau$}
        \State Discard update and use $\ell$ for prediction
      \Else
        \State Accept $\ell''$
      \EndIf
    \Else
      \State Accept $\ell'$
    \EndIf
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Conclusion}
SAUNet widens the scope of forward-only test-time adaptation beyond normalisation layers, removes the need for source data via synthetic self-supervision, introduces a hardware-friendly any-time compute knob and provides a provable \(\chi^2\) safety guard. Across six datasets and four diverse backbones it delivers state-of-the-art robustness at raw-inference speed with less than 0.1\,\% parameter overhead and a three-percent energy increase. Future work will explore group-wise statistics, extend the framework to dense prediction tasks and analyse the approximation error of Householder truncation under adversarial latency perturbations. We believe SAUNet paves the way for privacy-preserving, low-energy adaptive vision on mobile and safety-critical edge devices without compromising speed or stability.
