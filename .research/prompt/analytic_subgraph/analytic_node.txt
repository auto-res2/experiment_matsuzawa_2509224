────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Experiment 1 – End-to-End Scalability & Efficiency Benchmark

Goal:  Show that SAFE-VALS reaches hundred-million-scale while cutting cost.
Dataset:  ogbn-products (2.4 M), ogbn-papers100M (111 M) and synthetic       PowerLaw-100M (100 M).
Models:  2-layer GAT (8 h), 3-layer GATv2 (16 h).
Baselines:  Full GAT, GraphSAINT-GAT, BRSC-GAT, VALS-GAT, GST, NATTEN.
Hardware:  Single A100-80 GB; CPU inference on 16-core Xeon w/ 512 GB RAM.
Procedure:
 1. Train each model for 30 epochs (papers100M: 10 epochs) with AMP.
 2. Record: (i) wall-clock time/epoch, (ii) GPU & host peak memory, (iii)   floating-point Ops via NVProf, (iv) validation accuracy/ROC-AUC.
 3. After training, run full-graph inference once on GPU and once on CPU,   measure latency and memory.
 4. Repeat 5 seeds; report mean ± σ.
What success looks like:
 • ≥1.5× faster training on ogbn-products, ≥1.3× on papers100M.
 • ≥2.5× faster GPU inference; ≥3× faster CPU inference.
 • ≥45 % lower training memory; ≥60 % lower inference memory.
 • Accuracy within 0.3 % (ε = 0.05) of dense GAT.
Demonstrates:  raw speed/memory gains at “real-world” scale without accuracy loss; confirms claim that a pure-Python pipeline can run on a single A100.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Experiment 2 – Safety Guarantee & Automatic Fallback Stress Test

Goal:  Empirically validate the Bennett-tail bound and show that SAFE-VALS never violates the user-specified ε, δ while other sparsifiers do.
Dataset:  Reddit (231 k) for rapid iteration + ogbn-products for realism.
Setup:
 • Fix ε ∈ {0.01, 0.05}, δ = 10⁻⁶.
 • Insert an adversarial “burst” every 100 mini-batches: pick 1 % of nodes,   add Gaussian noise (σ = 2× feature std) and force them into one bucket to   maximise soft-max variance.
 • For each forward pass record:
     – max |ã̂ − ã| over all edges,  
     – whether fallback to dense attention was triggered,  
     – runtime overhead of safety check.
Compare to: VALS-GAT (expectation-only guarantee) and GST.
Success criteria:
 • SAFE-VALS: 0 violations in ≥10⁷ total attention scores; fallback rate ≤3 %; extra time ≤4 %.
 • Baselines: observe ≥10³ violations for ε = 0.05.
Demonstrates: worst-case-bounded behaviour and graceful degradation exactly when the bound would be broken—a key requirement for safety-critical use.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Experiment 3 – Adaptivity to Embedding Drift via Meta-Learned Hashing

Goal:  Show that the meta-learned hash h_θ tracks feature drift better than fixed LSH, yielding higher accuracy and lower FLOPs over long training horizons.
Dataset:  Temporal Reddit-Months (posts grouped by month, 12 batches).  After each month, node features are updated with new text embeddings; edges may also change (simulate social network drift).
Conditions:
 1. SAFE-VALS (full) – adaptive h_θ + periodic PQ distillation.
 2. SAFE-VALS-static – h_θ frozen after epoch 1 (mimics classic LSH).
 3. VALS-GAT – original cosine LSH.
Metrics per month:
 • Test ROC-AUC.
 • Average bucket purity:  fraction of top-K ground-truth high-attention   neighbours that fall in the elected bucket.
 • Training FLOPs.
Procedure:
 Train for 5 epochs on month t, then roll features/edges to month t+1 and continue (continual learning).  Run 3 seeds.
Success indicators:
 • Adaptive SAFE-VALS sustains ROC-AUC drop ≤0.5 % across months; static   LSH degrades by ≥2 % by month 12.
 • Bucket purity for adaptive version stays ≥85 %, static falls to <60 %.
 • Despite extra hash MLP, FLOPs are ≤10 % higher than static early on and   ≥15 % lower after month 4 due to better sparsity.
Demonstrates: ability to follow embedding drift and reclaim compute later, directly addressing Open Problem 1 (non-adaptive sparsifiers).

Together these three experiments attack the method’s purported strengths from orthogonal angles—scalability, safety, and adaptivity—yielding a comprehensive validation of SAFE-VALS.
